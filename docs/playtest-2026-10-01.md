# Bot playtest, levels 1-10 (2026-10-01)

An overnight playtest of all 40 shifts by a headless bot (`tools/playtest_bot.gd`),
with fixes applied between runs. Nothing is committed: review with `git diff`.

## How it was tested

The bot plays the real game, headless, at 4x speed. It walks, rakes, banks loads at the skip, dodges
tourists, buys upgrades in order and keeps one upgrade per finished level, as
the campaign does. It gets 3 attempts per shift; after that the shift is
skipped. Each level starts with the upgrades a real campaign would have kept by
then (rake, backpack, waders, jacket, tractor, sorter, sand tires, hopper,
diesel, in that order).

**Read it as a stress test, not a human.** It sees the whole beach (night
darkness doesn't blind it) and reacts instantly. But it is a poor dodger
(about 13 tourist hits per attempt) and a greedy planner. Treat a bot clear as
"a competent player can clear this". Treat a bot failure as "worth playing
yourself", not proof that it's impossible.

To rerun it: `sh tools/run_bots.sh <outdir> 4` (all levels, from shift 1) or
`sh tools/run_shifts.sh <outdir> 4 900 "6:3 10:4"` (jump straight into
level:shift pairs). Both point saves at a separate `SeaweedShiftBot` folder
while they run. Output is one JSON line per attempt.

## Global changes (affect the whole game)

Shift 1 of level 1 is mathematically unchanged by every one of these except
the first bug fix. Each change is commented in place with the playtest reason.

1. **Bug: dropped loads spawned during a physics callback** (`game.gd`,
   `on_player_hit`). A tourist hit fires inside `body_entered`, and adding the
   scattered piles there is refused by the physics server. 129 errors in one
   6-minute run, and the piles came out with dead collision shapes, possibly
   impossible to rake. Now `call_deferred`.

2. **Bug: `project.godot` was mangled by the editor.** The `#` comments in the
   `[display]` section had been rewritten into a garbage key that swallowed
   `window/stretch/aspect="keep"`. Restored the setting. Godot's config files
   only support `;` comments. The rationale that comment held (why "keep" and
   not "expand") is worth keeping in a doc instead.

3. **The storm surge is capped by units, not just clumps** (`spawner.gd`). The
   seaweed cap counted clumps, but storm clumps carry 2-3 units from shift 2
   on, so a storm filled the same cap with up to 3x the seaweed. 40-117 units
   would beach at once against a firing line of 28. A second cap now limits
   total units to 1.25x the clump cap.

4. **Reputation's firing line scales with the beach's seaweed cap**
   (`game.mess_full()`, `reputation.gd`). The cap rises from 34 to 65 clumps
   through the campaign, while "filthy" stayed fixed at 28 units, so late
   shifts let more seaweed on the beach than you could survive. It's now
   28 x cap/34. Level 1 shift 1 is exactly 28.

5. **Retries ease the cap, not just the spawn rate** (`game.cap_scale()`).
   Adaptive difficulty and Holbox's `spawn_scale` only slowed spawning. Once
   the beach sits at its cap, that changes nothing, so a failed shift was
   retried at the same pressure. Both now lower the cap too. They only ever
   ease: a clean run speeds spawning but never raises the cap.

6. **The tourist crowd grows with the square root of difficulty past shift 1**
   (`spawner.tourist_pressure()`). The crowd scaled linearly with difficulty:
   by level 10 shift 4 a new tourist arrived every 0.3-0.6 s, with 15+ on the
   sand at once. Identical up to difficulty 1.75, which covers all of level 1
   shift 1.

7. **A collision spills at most 16 units back onto the sand** (`game.gd`,
   `SPILL_MAX`). With the 100-unit hopper a full spill was bigger than the
   firing line on its own, so one hit could end a late shift. The whole load
   is still lost: the rest vanishes with its pay. Only the beach damage is
   capped.

8. **Storm drift is capped when it stacks with late-shift wash**
   (`seaweed.gd`, `STORM_DRIFT_MAX`). The 2.5x storm boost multiplied the
   late shifts' faster drift, up to 5-7x shift 1's speed. That landed the
   whole floating raft in 10-15 s, at the end of the first storm, on most
   attempts. The combined speed is now capped at 3.5x. Shift 1 keeps its full
   2.5x.

9. **Shift 4 difficulty 3.3 → 2.9** (`levels.gd`). It was the biggest step on
   the curve, on the shift meant to be the reward. Shift 3 is 2.6.

## Per level

### 1. Cancun
- **Changed:** nothing level-specific.
- **Result:** all 4 shifts clear. Shift 1 sometimes takes 2-3 attempts: on
  foot without the jacket, the first storm (75 s in) is the one real danger.
  That's the calibrated feel, so it was left alone.
- **Suggest:** shift 1 runs about 6-6.5 bot minutes, a bit over your 5-minute
  target. A human will probably take longer.

### 2. Veracruz
- **Changed:** nothing level-specific.
- **Result:** all 4 clear on the first attempt in both final passes.
- **Suggest:** shift 1 is the slowest shift in the game (7 bot minutes). The
  wind is fighting an on-foot worker the whole time. If it drags in hand,
  lower this level's shift 1 target; otherwise it's just the level's
  character.

### 3. Playa del Carmen
- **Changed:** nothing level-specific (the global fixes cleared it).
- **Result:** all 4 clear on the first attempt in both passes.
- **Suggest:** the ferry's shift 3/4 share (60%/80% of floating seaweed
  ashore per wake) is now survivable, but it is the level's whole difficulty
  spike. Worth playing yourself to judge the feel.

### 4. Cozumel (night)
- **Changed:** nothing level-specific.
- **Result:** shifts 1-3 clear first time; shift 4 needs 2-3 attempts.
- **Caveat:** the bot ignores the darkness, so this level is certainly harder
  for a human than the table shows. It's the level that most needs a human
  pass.

### 5. Bacalar
- **Changed:** nothing level-specific.
- **Result:** shifts 1-3 clear first time; shift 4 on attempt 2.

### 6. Isla Holbox
- **Changed:**
  - **No collisions inside the village** (`tourist.gd`). The streets are
    narrower than the tractor, and tourists walk out of the plaza down the
    same streets you must use to reach the skip. 35% of Holbox's load-dropping
    hits were there, with no way round. Tourists now "step into a doorway"
    while you're inside the village; the beach and water are unchanged.
  - The global cap fix (5) makes Holbox's 0.75 `spawn_scale` actually apply in
    shifts 2-4. Before, it only slowed spawning on a beach already at its cap.
- **Result:** shifts 1-2 clear first time. **Shift 3 not cleared** (95-96%
  of target, twice). **Shift 4 cleared once in two passes** (attempt 3).
- **Suggest:** the bot earns about half its usual rate here. The ring
  coastline is long, every trip goes through a street, and deep kelp
  surrounds everything. The design doc says Holbox should be a change of
  pace, not harder, so I'd lower its shift 3/4 credit targets, or its
  `spawn_scale` to about 0.6. I didn't, because the 95% results may just be
  the bot's poor routing on a radial map. Play it first.

### 7. Puerto Morelos
- **Changed:** nothing level-specific. (The bot had to learn to lean into
  the current. Seaweed collects at the stream mouth, and a worker standing
  still there is carried off its pile. A human pushes against it naturally,
  but it's worth knowing that the best piles sit in the current.)
- **Result:** all 4 clear, occasionally on attempt 2.

### 8. Mahahual
- **Changed:** nothing level-specific.
- **Result:** all 4 clear.
- **Suggest:** shifts 1-2 are very short (1.4-3 minutes). From level 5 on, the
  kept tractor makes the on-foot shift 1 nearly trivial. See the global
  suggestions.

### 9. Akumal
- **Changed:** nothing level-specific. (The bot needed pathing round the nest
  corners. The nests themselves behave correctly.)
- **Result:** shifts 1-3 clear first time; shift 4 cleared in one of the two
  passes (79% in the other).
- **Note:** stranded hatchlings cost 4 reputation each (32 for a full nest),
  and a hatching during a storm surge is what tipped the failed shift 4s. That
  feels like the intended drama.

### 10. Tulum
- **Changed:**
  - **Hurricane storms use shift 1's storm spawning on every shift**
    (`game.gd`, `HURRICANE_STORM_*`). The hurricane holds a storm for about 60%
    of the shift, but used each shift's spawn rate, which was tuned for a
    22-second burst.
  - **No storm drift boost under a hurricane** (`seaweed.gd`). The hurricane
    brings its own surf; both together emptied the whole water onto the sand
    within seconds of the front arriving.
  - **Surf share from shift 3: 60/80% → 40/50%** (`beaches.gd`).
  - Tried and reverted: `spawn_scale` 0.85 (no measurable effect).
- **Result:** shift 1 clears; shift 2 clears on attempt 2 (previously never).
  **Shift 3 cleared in one pass of two. Shift 4 not cleared** (53-58%).
  Failures land in the front (35-60% of target): gale gusts of 92-137 px/s,
  fast rot (shift 4 rot scale 0.58), surf, and a crowd lingering in the rain.
- **Suggest, in order of how much they'd change the feel:**
  1. Start the front later (`"front": 0.45`) so the eye comes sooner after it.
  2. Make the eye longer (20 s → 30 s). It's the only breather and the bot
     always arrives at it already failing.
  3. Slower rot during the hurricane (the rain keeps it wet?), e.g. rot_scale
     no lower than 0.75 on Tulum.
  4. Accept it: a finale a strong human clears on the second or third try may
     be exactly right. Play it before deciding.

## Suggestions not implemented (whole game)

- **Shift 1 gets trivial from level 5.** With the tractor kept, shift 1 of
  levels 7-10 lasts 1.5-2.4 minutes, against 5-7 in levels 1-4. Scaling credit
  targets by level (say +10% a level) would keep shifts near 5 minutes. The
  same retained upgrades do nothing for shift 4, which has the full kit
  anyway. That's why late-level shift 4s felt hardest.
- **Collisions are the biggest source of frustration.** Even after the fixes,
  the bot takes about 13 hits per shift. A human will do better, but the
  tourist hitbox (16x24) against the hopper (32x48) is a big target. Consider
  a brief grace period after banking, or letting tourists shy away from a
  moving tractor.
- **Rot weight (6x) is the hidden killer in every late failure.** On Tulum,
  only 15-25 units on the beach read as 50-80 "mess" because they had rotted.
  If late shifts still feel unfair in hand, lift `rot_scale` before touching
  anything else.
- **Storm on foot in level 1 shift 1.** The bot buys the rake, then saves for
  the backpack, so it meets the first storm without a jacket. If new players do
  the same, consider a one-line shop hint ("storm coming: the jacket helps").
- **`tools/smoke_test.gd` wipes the real save.** It calls `SaveGame.wipe()`
  against `user://`, which is your actual save folder. The bot runners redirect
  saves with a temporary `override.cfg`; the smoke test could do the same.
